# Sown Order

*A visual philosophy for documents that must persuade quietly and be believed.*

**Ground and seed.** Every composition starts as a field: a warm, paper-coloured ground, ruled by an invisible agricultural grid. The smallest unit is the dot, a perfect circle placed at an exact interval. One dot means nothing. Rows of dots become a plot, plots become a field, and when a few dots change colour or begin to converge, the eye reads intention. Information is shown as growth: scattered inputs settle into rows, rows feed channels, channels arrive at a single point. The work should feel as though it was laid out by hand with a ruler and a compass over many patient evenings, every dot counted twice.

**Colour as soil, crop and harvest.** The palette is deliberately agrarian and strictly limited: a deep paddy green for structure and authority; a muted sage for supporting marks; a single ripened ochre kept for the one thing on each page that matters most; near-black ink for words. Ochre is rare. If it appears more than a few times on a page it stops meaning "harvest" and becomes decoration. Restraint here is a matter of craft: each colour placement is weighed, and nothing is tinted only because it can be.

**Typography as specimen labelling.** Words behave like labels in a herbarium. A classical serif, sometimes in italic, carries the few phrases that must be felt, set large and calm with generous air around them. A plain grotesque carries short explanatory lines, never more than a breath long. A monospaced face supplies the clinical apparatus: plate numbers, coordinates, time stamps, codes. Text is never a wall. It sits in measured columns, aligned to the same grid as the dots, so that type and diagram read as one system made by one careful hand.

**Scale and rhythm.** The page alternates between the monumental and the minute: one large title against fields of tiny marks; a single heavy rule against hairlines. Rhythm comes from repetition at fixed intervals, such as dots at equal pitch, timelines in equal steps and columns of equal measure. Tension comes from the one place where the rhythm deliberately breaks. Negative space is not leftover. It is fallow ground left on purpose so the planted areas can be read.

**Composition as cartography.** Each page is a plate in an atlas of an imaginary discipline that studies how decisions travel through an organisation. Flows run in one direction, bottom to top or left to right, and every connector is drawn with the precision of a survey line. Reference markers such as plate numbers, figure labels and small coordinates suggest that the subject has been measured, not illustrated. Nothing overlaps or touches the edge, and every gap is intentional. The finished artefact should read as the product of deep expertise and painstaking attention: master-level execution that rewards a second, slower look.
